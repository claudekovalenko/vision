The growing edge

Walking a decision through

Five gates, in order. Most decisions are settled by gate 1 or 2 — and a feeling is never the first gate.

Gate 1

Has God already spoken on this?

Is it commanded, forbidden, or clearly addressed in Scripture?

If yes, this isn’t discernment — it’s obedience, and the decision is already made.

“Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path.” — Ps 119:105

Gate 2

Is it wise?

What would a wise man do here — given my season, my strength, my money, my time?

Where Scripture is silent, God rules by wisdom, not by hidden messages.

“If any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask God.” — James 1:5

Gate 3

Does it serve the callings?

Remaining in Jesus · a husband like Christ · disciples into His image. Which one does this build?

If it builds none of them, a good opportunity is still a no.

Gate 4

What do wise people around me say?

Who have I actually asked — mentors, guy friends, people who know me and will tell me no?

God most often speaks through counsel. Unasked counsel is the commonest reason for a wrong call.

“In an abundance of counselors there is safety.” — Prov 11:14

Gate 5

What is my heart doing?

After prayer, is there peace or unrest? And honestly — is this people-pleasing, comparison, greed, or wanting my own honor?

The heart comes last, not first. It confirms; it doesn’t decide.

“Let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts.” — Col 3:15

Then decide

God’s will is more often walked into than figured out. Where the gates are clear, decide — and don’t reopen it next week because the feeling moved.
